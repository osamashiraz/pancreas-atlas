# How the project got here (summary)

1. Organ: HRA reference pancreas regions; inventory with evidence levels and statuses; ontology copied from HRA with errors listed.
2. Extraction of ducts, vessels, duodenum and spleen from the HRA united models, bytes unchanged; loader through the bounded fetch.
3. Simulated vessels on landmark rules; grid shortest-path routing replaced point pushing after contacts and kinks; arcades close.
4. Splenic branches and lymph node stations with nearest-free placement.
5. Representative islet calibrated to Cabrera's 71% (random intermixing suffices once cells pack as tissue); islet distribution through the organ.
6. Tissue block: tube acini (21%) → rounded acini → overlap bug found (reported 66% was false) → fill pass → deformation by power diagram (52%).
7. Viewer: organ-to-cell zoom → continuous nested zoom → stages with hysteresis → structure alone, cells separated, one cell → duct branch → slice apart, names, picking, motion, journey → regions and gross level → 60 sites and generated blocks/islets → split screen, voice → 3D H&E with cut-face fill and stain grain → cell-type stain for all structures → professional voices, narration from StatPearls and histology sources, journey continues during interaction, cut open off by default. → lobule level shows the duct hierarchy (large, interlobular, intralobular, terminal ducts) with acinar clusters and scattered islets.

IPMN pins 3, 6 and 7 moved onto the mucin, the lining and the stroma. The KRAS figure on the PDA precursors page has a plain-language key above it (KRAS, GTP on and GDP off, GAP, G12D). 37. Atlas quiz: questions now come from a shuffled deck, so no entry is asked again until every entry in the chosen group has been asked (it used to draw at random with replacement, so the same question could come straight back); answer choices use a fair shuffle; an answer can only be scored once. Embedded figures report their height to the app, so each card shows the whole figure with no inner scrollbar. Histology pictures enlarged again (cards 351 px). Dead code from the old 2D schematic removed (about 90 lines of app.js, 22 CSS rules). 32. Tabs rebuilt around the interactive figures (reviewed by cell, molecular and cancer biology agents). 27. Mouse models vs human disease: a comparison entry at the top of the mouse group, written from the Discussion sections of Hingorani et al. 2003 (KC), 2005 (KPC) and Izeradjene et al. 2007 (KDC), now read in full from the lab-publications PDFs. It sets out what the models reproduce (PanIN precursors and the full grade range; metastatic sites; chromosomal instability; Kras+Smad4 cystic MCN-like tumours) and where they differ (instability without telomere attrition, unlike human; mouse resistance to complex epithelial tumours; compressed timescale; each genotype one facet). A small two-column like/unlike block renders it.

22. Faster rendering, measured with a new profiler (viewer/test/profile.py: draw calls, triangles and frame times at each stage, optionally at 2× pixel density). The tissue core was drawing the whole 300 µm block and discarding most of it in the shader: the block now keeps only what can lie in the core (Tissue level 9.6 M → 2.3 M triangles; 13 fps before, now 45–60 fps depending on the site, also at 2×). Gross acini and the block's duct cells use lighter shapes (Lobules 2.1 M → 0.7 M triangles). On dense (Retina) screens multisampling is off, and a single cell no longer draws its islet at 5% behind it unless "Show the surrounding tissue" is on: beta cell 29 → about 58 fps, duct cell 52 → 60 fps at 2×. Dynamic resolution lowers the pixel ratio (never below 1×) while frames run slow and raises it again when there is room. Dead code (old weights, anchors and levels, an unused helper, unused text) removed.
